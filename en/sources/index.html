Sources & method

Each issue is built from the full in-period corpus—not a few convenient examples. Publisher fame alone never makes an idea important.

  1. 01

    Check every source

    Every active source receives an independent status: publications found, no new material, unverified date, or a local error.

  2. 02

    Retrieve the full text

    We use the official channel and fallback routes only for the same public link already found. One failed item never stops the issue.

  3. 03

    Extract verifiable ideas

    The event is separated from its mechanism. Every substantial conclusion retains a link to the material and its specific evidence.

  4. 04

    Build semantic themes

    Supporting evidence, limits, and contradictions are grouped around one question—not around a publisher's name.

  5. 05

    Rank against context

    Importance depends on novelty, mechanism strength, evidence quality, and the ability to change understanding—not publisher fame.

  6. 06

    Construct the reader's narrative

    The full analytical layer becomes a coherent issue, with a few deep links only where further reading is genuinely worthwhile.

  7. 07

    Verify the evidence

    A fresh verifier checks every sentence against its exact evidence, including names, quantities, scope, modality, links, and the absence of private data.

  8. 08

    Polish the finished issue

    A separate editorial pass rebuilds each block answer-first, sharpens the Russian prose, and removes formulaic constructions. Every rewritten sentence is then checked against the evidence again.

Independent research, podcasts, M&A practice, and product-model analysis—only when a concrete mechanism or evidence is present.

Set

Video and conversational formats

YouTube and podcasts are analyzed from captions or transcripts, not only from titles and descriptions.

Selection check

How we verify that the issue contains what truly matters

  1. Lose nothing. Every extracted claim receives an explicit status and stays linked to an exact quotation. No claim remains unreviewed when an issue is released.
  2. Assemble ideas. Evidence, objections, and limits for one idea stay together. Publisher prestige and repetition from one source add no weight.
  3. Choose what matters. Ideas are compared by novelty, consequence, evidence strength, specificity, and relevance. The weakest included claim is then checked against the strongest deferred one, and every public sentence returns to its source for a final verification.
