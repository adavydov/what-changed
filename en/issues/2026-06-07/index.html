Infrastructure, context, and reliability determine AI’s value

New data and research converge on one point: AI adoption cannot be assessed by the number of accounts and launches. What matters is the quality of context, a system’s ability to retain state, the infrastructure through which it is used, and the consequences for risk.

Published · 10–12 min read
Chapter 01

Main changes

Story 01

Growing use of AI in accounting is not visible in conventional adoption metrics

In accounting work, AI is taking on a growing share of the process, while employee access to tools, system logins, and usage reflect the transformation itself poorly.

According to Accounting Today, AI is absorbing a growing share of the accounting workflow each quarter. Deloitte notes that deployed copilots, employee access, system logins, and usage are weak indicators of a company’s transformation.

In other words, growth in tool use and a change in the process are different things; the sources are specifically discussing accounting work and enterprise AI adoption.

Story 02

Scaling models does not solve the problem of agent resilience

In one study, agent resilience improved with model scale more slowly than overall task performance; for search success, retaining state mattered more than search volume.

The authors of a dynamic replanning benchmark report that agent resilience improves with model scale 3.66 times more slowly than overall task performance. In the K-BrowseComp study, search volume was a weak predictor of success; a stronger signal was the model’s ability to retain candidate, constraint, and role states across searches.

Together, these results limit conclusions about agent readiness: strong task performance and a high number of searches do not by themselves describe stable system behavior.

Story 03

AI’s value depends on preparing context, not only on the model

No Priors argues that the difficulty of implementation needed to achieve real value has been underestimated, and calls preparing the context layer a key source of impact.

In an episode of No Priors, the source says that the difficulty of implementing systems in the real world to generate value has probably been underestimated. The same source calls the work of preparing the context layer, so that a plan can be executed most effectively, a key source of impact.

This is not a measured universal recipe, but an attributed assessment from the source: the main work may lie in configuring the execution environment rather than choosing the model itself.

Story 04

Risk is determined by the infrastructure around the model, not simply by the use of AI

In Anthropic’s research, the share of actors rated at least moderate for AI use rose; a durable indicator of high risk was identified as infrastructure that connects stages of a cyberattack with minimal human involvement.

In Anthropic’s research, the share of actors rated at least moderate for AI use rose from about 33.5% in the first six months of the study to about 56.1% in the second, a 1.7-fold increase in less than a year. The authors call infrastructure around the model a more durable marker of high-risk actors: it makes it possible to connect separate stages of a cyberattack and carry them out with minimal human involvement.

Therefore, within this study, what matters more is not the mere fact that an attacker uses AI, but the design of the system in which the model can sequentially carry out parts of an attack.

Story 05

AI infrastructure has become the leading driver of private investment growth in the United States

Epoch AI reports that AI infrastructure has become the leading driver of private investment growth in the United States, and that its boom has increased computing infrastructure’s share of the country’s GDP.

According to Epoch AI, AI infrastructure has become the leading driver of private investment growth in the United States. Epoch AI also reports that the AI infrastructure boom, which began in 2023, has more than doubled the share of US GDP accounted for by computing infrastructure.

This macroeconomic measure applies specifically to the United States and to Epoch AI’s metric, so it should not be read as an estimate of global investment in AI.

Story 06

Test paraphrases of documentation do not replace user queries

The ToolSense authors describe a gap between ToolBench queries resembling API documentation and short formulations of user intent; they do not recommend making production-deployment decisions without independent validation.

ToolBench queries are verbose narrative formulations that effectively paraphrase API documentation for the same tools. The ToolSense authors argue that the shift to short conversational queries expressing user intent creates a distribution gap: a parametric model trained on stage-2 pairs of verbose ToolBench queries did not overcome this lexical gap.

The authors explicitly do not recommend using such benchmarks for production-deployment decisions without independent validation on the target tool catalog.

Story 07

How Lagercrantz manages a portfolio of 85 companies

Lagercrantz Group CEO Jörgen Wigh told M&A Science that a portfolio of 85 autonomous companies cannot fit within the personal knowledge of an owner or analyst, so the group distributed leadership by country.

Lagercrantz Group CEO Jörgen Wigh told M&A Science that the group’s portfolio includes 85 companies and cannot be covered by the personal knowledge of an owner or analyst. Lagercrantz therefore appointed country managers while retaining a distributed management model.

This is an observation about Lagercrantz’s structure; it does not prove that distributed management suits every owner of a group of companies.

Story 08

LatentSkill transfers textual skills into plug-in adapters

The authors introduced LatentSkill, which converts textual skills into LoRA adapters; on the Pick2 task, the method scored higher than the second-best method.

LatentSkill converts textual skills into plug-in LoRA adapters using a pretrained hypernetwork. On the previously unseen Pick2 task, LatentSkill reached 70.6%, outperforming the second-best method by 41.2 points.

This result applies to the Pick2 task specified by the authors and characterizes their evaluation of the method, not every agent deployment scenario.
