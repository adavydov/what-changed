Chapter 01

How an agent acts in finance

Story 01

One agent step ends in a new record

The path from order to payment passes through several records: an order becomes an invoice and reaches the customer. When payment arrives, it must be linked to an obligation. An error along this path enters revenue, the cash forecast, and the customer conversation.

Deloitte treats the process as a chain of states. AI reads remittance documents and finds the invoice before estimating payment timing and drafting a reminder. It also helps calculate a revenue accrual. In one project, that calculation fell from hours to minutes, with each step leaving a controlled change inside the active finance system.

The system creates a record for each step, starting with the initial state. The permitted action sits beside it; evidence is added after completion. Before cash application, the agent receives the bank transaction, remittance document, and candidate invoices. The final record keeps the amount with its currency, the selected invoice, and a source link, while a person receives an ambiguous case with the evidence already assembled.

These records can move across a group of accounting firms because the shared system knows the states and core constraints. Every client keeps a separate version of its rules. It contains the materiality threshold and approval path. Overdue period and acceptable variance are stored there as well. Recurring exceptions enter a shared set of tests.

Deloitte surveyed 662 leaders at large enterprises, with 42% expecting AI to enter more than 40% of processes by 2028. Today, 6% report that level. Companies most often revisit the design of work every quarter. The financial agent's owner opens the records on the same rhythm, finds growth in the exception queue, and examines expensive rules. A decision follows on which stable step can receive more freedom.

One record shows review cost and how often work comes back. Time to a confirmed state captures delay, with loss from error stored beside it. A chain with poor input documents gains most from extraction. Manual cash application improves when payment matching becomes reliable.

Story 02

Agent safety shows up in the system record

After a dangerous tool call, an agent may report that it refused the action. A confident success message can leave the system unchanged. The verifier reads the consequences.

The VERA researchers turned this check into executable tests, each starting from an exact environment state with observable harm defined in advance. The program reads the final record first, then opens the tool log and the agent's response. A transfer is confirmed in the payment system. Changed data appears in a new record version.

A harmful instruction can enter through ordinary work material by hiding in an email or search result. Sometimes it arrives inside a payment document; an external service response opens another channel. VERA injects such instructions into tool results and runs the attack across several turns. In four production agent frameworks, average attack success reached 93.9% in the multi-channel setting. The released set contains 1,600 scenarios across 124 risk categories.

A financial-agent test is built around an expected state. The sandbox contains a supplier with valid bank details, with an email beside it asking for a change. After receiving a routine document-processing task, the agent follows its working path. The verifier reads the supplier record and approval log before finding the outbound payment. Records in these systems decide the result.

Before changing the model, the team reruns the full scenario set; the same check follows an edited prompt or a newly connected system. Support messages add another environment to a familiar risk. A changed dual-approval rule updates the initial state and the check, with every run remaining available for replay.

Evidence of a safe action stays beside the accounting-system entry and outlives the model response. The team reads it before releasing a new version. The same object is opened during an incident and before an agent receives wider permission for a specific operation.

Chapter 02

People and the cost of work

Story 03

Future experts train on live exceptions

A junior accountant learns from a stream of simple transactions where strange documents and missing fields appear. Some decisions need revision. Over time, the accountant begins to feel an error before it reaches reporting.

Deloitte studied US job postings from 2022 through 2025 and assessed the automatability of 19,000 work tasks in O*NET. During that period, demand for junior data scientists and software developers fell. Senior demand held at its previous level. Automation reached beginner and intermediate tasks that once built experience.

Experts read an answer differently. They notice missing context and a hidden assumption. Sometimes the strongest signal is a document that should have been requested. A clean model answer can conceal that training case.

In a group of accounting firms, each live dispute becomes a short simulation. The employee chooses a treatment before seeing the senior's answer and marks the missing information. They then open the evidence, the experienced accountant's path, and the real outcome after close. The system stores the gap between the decisions.

The cases form an experience map. One employee has seen a legal-entity change midway through a contract. Another has handled returns with partial payment. The next assignment goes to the person with that gap. Signing authority grows with the number of situations completed successfully.

An agent correction also becomes training material when the senior identifies the missing evidence and records the reason for the decision. That trail enters product tests before appearing in the junior's explanation. The firm keeps the same case in memory. Access rights and local rules remain attached to the record.

Story 04

Sonnet 5 at maximum effort takes six times more turns

On GDPval-AA, Claude Sonnet 5 at maximum effort used roughly six times as many agent turns as low effort. Across agentic evaluations, it averaged about three times as many turns as Sonnet 4.6. The mode changes task cost before another model is selected.

Artificial Analysis calculated the average task cost for Sonnet 5 at maximum effort and standard pricing at $2.29. Sonnet 4.6 cost roughly half as much. The Sonnet 5 figure stood 15% above Opus 4.8.

Google DeepMind's research shows where the turns go. TRACE cuts a reasoning chain into short fragments and maps movement toward the answer. The graph reveals long detours into side branches. Repeated checks can continue after a solution appears. The utility of each step became the measure of unproductive reasoning.

The mode selector receives its own effort budget. A simple payment match with one candidate invoice fits into a short run with a precise state check. A new contract type allows a longer search. Irreversible movement of money receives separate spending for evidence and human approval.

Stopping depends on task movement. A new document may permit another turn. A changed leading option or narrower uncertainty also counts as progress. Repeating the previous reason ends the loop and sends the case to a person.

Tool work enters task cost first. A long context adds its own expense, and reviewer time is counted separately. The task profile stores the number of turns together with evidence cost. Return frequency sits beside them, so high effort receives a case after measured improvement on that class of work.
