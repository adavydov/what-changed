Chapter 01

Model price and business rules

Story 01

A model one point from the lead costs $1.04 per task

Four strong models arrived in eight days. Since early June, the number of labs above 50 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index has risen from two to six. Claude Fable 5 held the lead at 60. GPT-5.6 Sol scored 59, and Kimi K3 entered third at 57.

Price compressed faster than the ranking. An average Index task cost $2.75 on Fable 5 and $1.04 on GPT-5.6 Sol. A single point behind the leader saved $1.71. Kimi cut another ten cents with a three-point gap.

Kimi has a jagged profile. It placed second on AA-Briefcase for long-horizon office work and won the SaaS automation test. K3 answered 46% of knowledge questions correctly, up from 33% for K2.6. Fabricated answers appeared 51% of the time, compared with 39% for its predecessor.

Kimi was already available through its website, apps, and API that week. Moonshot scheduled the weights for July 27. The model contains 2.8 trillion parameters and activates 16 of 896 experts at each step. That size turns self-hosting into an infrastructure project.

An advantage based on access to a strong model now expires quickly. A release can move both price and ranking within days. An annual contract built around a markup on tokens loses its foundation as buyers compare it with a direct API call.

A vendor can sell an accepted reconciliation with a clear trail back to source documents. Its unit cost exposes model calls together with returns and review. Data access and authority to complete the entry retain value after the next drop in token prices.

A private set of real tasks records the cost of an accepted result and the character of each model’s errors. Procurement can use that history to assign a model to each operation.

Story 02

Maybern opened a fund’s calculation rules to agents

Maybern opened the engine that calculates a fund's fees, waterfalls, and performance to agents. Claude, ChatGPT, and a firm's own agents can connect through MCP. They receive live data together with the logic that turns fund documents into numbers.

The question “Why did carry move?” requires the fund’s rules to run again on live data. The answer emerges from its waterfall tiers and the special term granted to a particular investor.

Maybern stores those rules in MXL. Fee formulas, waterfall tiers, and side-letter interpretations become expressions. A person reviews each expression, then Maybern keeps its versions. The agent runs the relevant version on new data and exposes every calculation step.

An agent can save a calculation or report only as a draft. A Maybern user gives it final status.

Oracle opened a similar runtime inside Fusion that week. Agents inherit the accounting system’s permissions. Approvals follow existing routes, and every action enters the log. The same rules cover external agents.

Maybern’s value sits in MXL. It contains the firm’s executable interpretation of contracts and accounting policy. The agent applies that logic to a new question as the team would work it out by hand.

After an accounting acquisition, the group can move the firm’s calculation decisions into a shared layer and retain local exceptions. Each result links the rule version to its source documents and the employee who authorized the entry.

Chapter 02

Project memory and physical power

Story 03

HumanLayer moved project memory from chats into documents

In July 2025, Dex Horthy’s team ran an experiment: models wrote the code and people stopped reading it. Production broke four months later. Opus 4.1 searched repeatedly and missed the cause. People spent several days finding a bad primary key routed through the entire codebase.

The team then spent another three weeks relearning its own system. Its history lived in expired chats and code that no person had read. A new session saw the consequences of earlier choices without a clear account of why they existed.

Horthy now starts complex work in a dedicated session that reads the code and leaves a short account of the system. That document feeds the design. After a person reviews the architecture, another agent writes the plan and a fresh context receives the implementation.

Each session spends its context on one kind of work and leaves a document for the next agent. A person reads the design and checks the architecture. The accepted decision survives the chat in compact form.

Horthy opens a fresh session around 100,000 tokens for smaller models. He usually uses 300,000 to 400,000 tokens from a one-million-token window. In his experience, quality starts to fall beyond those points. His team uses them to choose when to reset the context.

Horthy estimates that full review of agent-written code produces a 30% to 50% speed gain. Keeping architecture with a person and handing implementation to an agent can reach two to three times. Four agents prepare four changes overnight, and a person reads every one in the morning.

In a financial project, these documents preserve the logic behind exceptions. A short record beside the change describes the system, chosen permissions, and checks required before an entry. The next employee can see why the process works this way.

Story 04

Lancium and Crusoe split the powered site from the data center

Lancium and Crusoe announced a new campus in Childress, Texas, on July 15. It will carry one gigawatt of capacity across 270 acres. The site can host hundreds of thousands of accelerators for training and inference.

Lancium owns the land, secures the grid connection, and manages energy. Crusoe designs, builds, and operates the computing facility. The companies already use this structure in Abilene. A powered site and the data center built on it have become separate products.

Epoch AI identifies grid connections as a major constraint on new construction. At Childress, Lancium owns that connection and manages the load. Behind-the-meter solar and storage will support the site.

Epoch AI estimates that power used to train a frontier model has roughly doubled each year. If the trend continues, one large training run could require about 10 gigawatts by the end of the decade. Several sites would need fiber links between them.

For a model supplier, the grid-connection date becomes part of the production plan. An enterprise buyer has a direct question: where is capacity reserved, and how long will a transfer take during an outage?

An enterprise contract should name the operating region and the time allowed to switch sites. Reserved capacity needs its own term because ordinary uptime can hide a shortage during a sudden load increase.
