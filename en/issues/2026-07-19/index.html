AI’s value is determined not by execution speed, but by verifiability and process design

AI tools are moving more quickly into workflows, vendor selection, and infrastructure, but value at scale emerges only where the work has been redesigned and results can be checked. Accenture’s research shows how rare that redesign is; in agentic purchasing, the final decision is also still rarely handed over to the system.

Chapter 01

Main changes

Story 01

AI value requires process redesign

Accenture reports that only a small share of companies have redesigned processes around AI tools. BCG AI Radar likewise finds that companies reporting value creation at scale are rare.

According to BCG, companies oriented around AI need an end-to-end redesign of how work gets done, not isolated use cases. BCG also notes that people and change management often receive too little attention and investment. In BCG’s account, leading boards supplement executive presentations with direct sessions with frontline employees to see AI deployment without a filter. Accenture argues that processes need to change so AI’s speed gains do not stall at approvals and control points.

BCG warns that simply adding AI to the old process delivers only part of the potential value. An Accounting Today author links individual employees’ independent experimentation to risks whose full consequences are still unknown. BCG notes that agentic solutions can be used by people other than software engineers, which puts the question of process design beyond the IT team.

Story 02

Automation compresses execution, but does not replace professional judgment

Arvind Narayanan describes a shrinking execution layer in software development: writing and debugging code are only part of the work.

Narayanan describes a shift in effort from direct execution to steering the work and choosing its goal. Automating routine tasks can free time for work that requires judgment. McKinsey points to the cost of that shift: many automatable tasks gave junior employees practice in building experience and professional judgment. As a16z puts it, flawless presentation does not rule out a confident mistake. Narayanan believes AI performs noticeably better on tasks whose results can be checked than on tasks without such verification. An Accounting Today example separates having documents from professionally analyzing them: ChatGPT’s output covers documentation, but does not replace the analytical work of a qualified professional.

As intermediate stages of development are automated, Every writes, the decisive factor remains human judgment: whether the finished product is good enough. Gergely Orosz described Dex Horthy’s experiment in development without human review; the system was later shut down and abandoned. Narayanan insists that human understanding remains an essential part of the aims of science. He also calls a future in which AI owns companies, runs them, and makes hiring decisions dystopian. As a historical qualification, Narayanan cites ATMs: they enabled banks to expand their branch networks, where people were still needed for work machines could not do; employment grew as a result.

Story 03

Agents connected to accounting systems bring technology players into the services market

The Accounting Podcast reported rapid customer connections from Xero to Claude and rising use of the Xero API after integrations with agents began.

Accounting Today estimates that the revenue under active review in the US CPA market is substantial. Future Firm notes the contested position of Intuit Expert Bookkeepers: a software vendor has entered the services market. Accounting Today reports that Ramirez created Four-Eye Check, a segregation-of-duties layer for AI-performed financial reconciliation.

When knowledge of how a system works and how to fix it is not distributed within the firm, there is a risk of having no backup.

Story 04

Agents are more dynamic than web apps, so model quality is no longer enough

Latent Space’s roundup identifies a shift: observability and the quality of the execution environment are becoming distinct differentiators for agent products.

BCG writes that, in an agentic operating model, traders and operators work less with fragmented systems and spend more time overseeing decisions. Latent Space warns that a model can miss an experiment and still produce the correct result, so an observable reasoning trace is not a reliable explanation. BCG notes that without feedback, versioned baselines, and continuous monitoring, drift in an agent’s behavior can go unnoticed until a business incident occurs.

Accenture says AI can produce production-ready code in very little time. At Cursor, Brunet says, the “software factory” means long-running agents that help people throughout the process.

Story 05

AI is becoming a new interface between buyers and brands

In Accenture’s study, most respondents expect generative AI to influence a substantial share of their spending decisions in the near term.

Accenture’s study covers a large international sample of consumers. Accenture reports that consumers are prepared to delegate decisions to AI agents, from individual tasks to autonomous purchases. At the same time, many consumers would prefer to specify for the agent which brands it should consider. For a notable share of respondents, budget and value are the main guides in instructions to an agent.

Loyalty remains conditional: some behaviorally loyal buyers would let an agent switch their preferred brand for a better fit with the request. Payments remain the main obstacle: few consumers are willing to let an agent make the final purchase decision. BCG believes agents can compare service outcomes, product availability, price accuracy, and customer reviews in real time. In BCG’s view, the same APIs and structured data flows that make a brand discoverable also make its performance relative to competitors measurable. McKinsey links differentiation to business-customer relationships amid greater choice, higher expectations, and buyers’ ability to make their views heard.

Story 06

Agentic tasks are changing the cost and demand for computing infrastructure

Micron CFO Mark Murphy said the company had moved its expectation for a major HBM market milestone to an earlier date.

Epoch AI reports that the electricity needs of leading AI supercomputers have been doubling rapidly. Micron CBO Sumit Sadana said demand for HBM capacity materially exceeds the company’s supply, describing customer requests over a long horizon. McKinsey disclosed a large monthly volume of AI-token processing inside the company. McKinsey stresses that architecture, model selection, context handling, routing, caching, evaluation, and autonomy directly affect the cost and performance of machine work.

Latent Space cites low average FLOP utilization in reinforcement learning as a qualification to straightforward calculations of hardware needs. An a16z author explains a possible price decline for payers through economies of scale: with greater demand, a network’s fixed costs are spread more widely. As an observed qualification, the same source points to Texas and Virginia: despite rapid growth in load and data centers, there was almost no increase in electricity prices there.

Story 07

Kimi K3 has become a notable open competitor, but its practical limits remain disputed

On an Artificial Analysis chart, Kimi K3 ranked highly among the compared models, ahead of Claude Opus and behind Claude Fable and GPT-5.6.

Some participants in the discussion believe open Chinese models are putting economic pressure on US labs, compressing margins and turning model capabilities into a more commoditized product. Assessments of Kimi K3 differ: some call the model close to the frontier in particular areas, while others see it lagging in generality, efficiency, or hidden evaluations. Skeptics note that self-hosting an open-weights model may still be affordable only for well-funded teams. Latent Space’s practical conclusion is narrower: Kimi K3 cannot be ignored.

Latent Space describes Kimi Delta Attention as a memory mechanism that maintains a learned fixed-size state for each query rather than paying the full cost of attention over long contexts. Moonshot AI promised to publish Kimi K3’s weights by the stated date. Moonshot also acknowledged a noticeable gap between Kimi K3 and Claude Fable and GPT-5.6 Sol in user experience.
