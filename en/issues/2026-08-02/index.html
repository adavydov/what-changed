Chapter 01

How tasks move beyond the job

Story 01

Workers started borrowing other people’s tasks

On July 27, OpenAI published an analysis of more than 800,000 work-related messages from U.S. ChatGPT users. 16.8% of all work messages and 43.5% of occupation-specific messages concerned a task normally associated with another occupation. The company calls this shift task crossover.

After generic activities such as writing and summarization were excluded, outside tasks made up 77% of occupation-specific messages from customer-experience workers, 75% from designers, 69% from HR, 56% from legal workers, and 53% from marketers. A handoff to a specialist is increasingly replaced by an attempt to complete the task near where it appears.

Financial calculation and technology troubleshooting were among the three most common outside tasks in all seven other occupational groups. Marketing and engineering tasks traveled farthest: AI was not only accelerating a function but making part of that function’s work accessible to its neighbors.

For average users, the outside-task share fell from 18.9% in workspaces with 2–5 seats to 16.3% in workspaces with more than 100 seats. OpenAI suggests that a small company is more likely to replace scarce specialist capacity with a general-purpose tool. The same monotonic relationship did not appear among the heaviest users.

A ChatGPT message is not completed work: a classifier can be wrong, and a draft does not prove quality or accepted responsibility. But the job is already an insufficient map of work. A borrowed task needs a passport: its source discipline, acceptable risk, verification criterion, reviewer, and mandatory handoff point.

Story 02

Work is changing inside old jobs

On July 28, Revelio Labs released its July AI Labor Market Tracker. Its central result for work content: 93% of activity change is happening within jobs, not through changes in the mix of jobs. A role’s name can remain stable while its daily work has already changed.

Since October 2022, demand for the most AI-exposed roles has declined 42% relative to the least exposed. At the same time, firms classified as AI adopters grew headcount 27% more than other firms. Within that group, senior roles grew 31%, while junior roles grew 6%.

Those numbers do not form a simple displacement story. Adopting firms were growing faster even before formal adoption, and measured cumulative adoption has only approached 5.9% of eligible hiring firms. Exposure represents expected pressure, while adoption represents observed deployment, and the two can move differently.

Revelio builds its measures from job postings, professional online profiles, and adoption proxies. These are observational data, not a causal experiment: they do not prove that AI created either growth or decline. But through an independent method, they show the same lagging boundary as the ChatGPT data.

The headcount plan is becoming a weak sensor of transformation. A manager needs a task-flow ledger: which task crossed a function, how much review it required, where the defect rate rose, and when the work returned to a specialist. Otherwise, the new organization remains invisible inside old titles.

Chapter 02

Where capability travels

Story 03

One brain gained several bodies

On July 30, Google DeepMind introduced Gemini Robotics 2 as an intelligence layer for multiple physical platforms. The family has three parts: a whole-body motor-control model, an agent for planning and communication, and an on-device model that can work without network latency.

One checkpoint controls multiple embodiments: a full-size humanoid and bi-arm robots with different grippers. Instead of separate logic for each hand, leg, and platform, the model transfers a vision-language representation of a task into the movement of a specific body.

The embodied-reasoning model executes sequences lasting several minutes and hundreds of decisions, marks the beginning and end of stages, and corrects a failed step. DeepMind also demonstrated robots of different types collaborating on one workflow.

The on-device model can be adapted to a new bi-arm platform in a few hours, typically with fewer than 200 examples. A separate safety layer tests refusal of unsafe tool calls, feasibility assessment, and requests for human help under uncertainty.

These are developer demos and metrics, not an independent industrial trial; access remains in trusted tester and private preview programs. But the deployment unit is becoming a shared capability layer plus a body passport: sensors, motion limits, safe-stop behavior, and authority to act.

Story 04

A document infected the next document

On July 28, researcher Håkon Måløy disclosed a new class of attack against Copilot for Word. A hidden instruction in an external document could make Copilot alter a report being drafted and silently copy the same instruction into the output.

The new file became a carrier. When an employee used it as a source for another report, the instruction triggered again even though the original malicious document was no longer present. In the proof of concept, Copilot changed financial figures and appended the attack in white text.

An attacker does not need access to the victim’s tenant. The document must enter Copilot’s context through an attachment or a search for relevant files. After the first transfer, the internal file appears trusted and can travel through ordinary SharePoint, Teams, or email routes.

The disclosure was coordinated with Microsoft for 144 days. Two mitigation attempts, including a model change, closed specific phrasings, but at publication the researcher could reproduce the broader mechanism with a modified payload. Microsoft confirmed the original behavior and worked on mitigations.

This is a laboratory scenario, not evidence of a mass campaign. The practical conclusion already extends beyond Word: a generated file does not inherit its author’s trust. It needs source provenance, a log of model edits, inspection of hidden content, and quarantine before reuse as context.

Story 05

Code review gained organizational memory

On July 29, GitHub made agent skills and MCP for Copilot code review generally available on Pro, Pro+, Business, and Enterprise plans. The automated reviewer can now rely not only on the diff and repository, but also on explicitly connected team rules and work systems.

A SKILL.md file under .github/skills adds standards and instructions for a particular repository or organization. MCP connections pull context from issue trackers, documentation, and service catalogs. Settings already configured for the cloud agent also apply to code review.

GitHub limits MCP tool calls in review to read-only access. A comment also includes attribution when skill or MCP context helped generate it. The provenance of a finding moves from a hidden inference detail into a visible part of the review.

The release proves availability, not quality. GitHub does not publish precision, recall, acceptance rates, or effects on defects. A poor internal rule can now scale just as easily as a good one.

Reviewer memory therefore needs to be a versioned product: a rule owner, scope, source standard, test violations, and review date. Read-only limits tool action, and attribution explains context, but neither proves that the advice is correct.

Chapter 03

What remains after implementation

Story 06

Scientific code became a stewardship problem

On July 28, OpenAI published a field report on eight agent-assisted scientific-computing projects, primarily in the life sciences. Five used Codex, while three used Codex together with Claude Code. The work included packaging, optimization, language migration, and GPU redesign.

A recurring shift: the researcher implements less and spends more time defining the goal, decomposing the work, and judging scientific validity. Agents produced a first implementation quickly, but edge cases and subtle numerical differences made the last mile the hardest part.

The strongest checks did not ask whether the model was confident. Teams compared results with an external reference, required exact parity with an existing tool, tested expected statistical behavior, or used answers established in advance on simulated data.

Lower implementation cost creates another risk: several similar rewrites fragment users and expert attention. Changes to cyvcf2 and MHCflurry entered their original upstream projects; the abandoned rustar-aligner needed a new community steward.

These are retrospective case studies without a control group, so they do not measure general productivity uplift or scientific outcomes. But the operational bottleneck is visible: new code needs an owner, scientific invariants, an upstream route, and a maintenance plan. Otherwise, a cheap modernization becomes the next abandoned tool.
