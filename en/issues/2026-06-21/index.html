Chapter 01

Rebuilding the work

Story 01

BCG’s end-to-end AI programs delivered three times the productivity gain

BCG traces the gap between 70% adoption and 16% significant value to the design of daily work.

An agent in an old process speeds up one section until the queue reaches the same exception path. Total productivity gains in such projects land at 10% to 20%. BCG's first end-to-end programs delivered three times the productivity; cycle time fell by 80% and long-term cost by at least 60%.

A European bank built loan processing around the completed decision. An agent recognizes documents and moves the data into the system. Separate checks look for fraud and validate signatures. Automation covered more than 90% of consumer loans and over 70% of mortgages, with process productivity rising by more than 50%.

At Siemens, the redesign began with a map of the old system. Hundreds of millions of lines of code sat beside Jira tickets, Confluence pages, and scanned manuals. Fragment search lost the links between a requirement, a module, and a line of code.

Knowledge Fabric turns those links into a graph. Search brings up the relevant nodes, then a product owner answers an agent's questions and writes the acceptance criteria. Another agent estimates the architectural impact. The prepared task already contains its context. A person accepts every transition after the code is written.

In a group of accounting firms, a similar map connects the client to the legal entity, account, and document. The record also holds the firm's rule, a prior exception, and the person who made the decision. The agent receives this packet for one close, including the boundary of the change and the outcome check.

BCG recommends choosing two or three domains that move a business metric. In an accounting firm, that unit becomes one closed month for one client. An owner carries it from document collection through approval. Each week the team measures close time, review minutes, reopened work, and margin per client.

Story 02

MCP adds centrally managed access for connectors

The system grants an agent permission for one action and sets the expiry at the same time. Access disappears when the work is done.

On June 18, MCP added an extension called Enterprise-Managed Authorization. The company's identity system now grants access to an agent's connectors. An employee signs in through Okta, and Claude receives the rights attached to that person's group and role. A role change makes the old token expire quickly. A single revocation closes access through every supported tool.

Anthropic launched it for Asana, Atlassian, Canva, Figma, Granola, Linear, and Supabase. In a group of accounting firms, it replaces personal permissions scattered across employee laptops. The group first chooses which firm's system the agent may enter. Reading an invoice inside that system gets a separate permission. Saving a draft and posting the entry require their own permissions.

Deloitte embedded such a system into a lending process. Control agents watch the work, inspect the result, and stop a risky action during execution. Every check has a known target and evaluation rule. Failure follows a predefined route. Ordinary code handles stable rules, and a model reads situations whose meaning depends on context.

Google DeepMind builds access as a trust ladder. Verified behavior unlocks a wider scope. Reading and other reversible actions can be checked later. Data deletion and infrastructure changes wait for review before execution. The control system is measured by traffic coverage, detected violations, and response time. Its first dataset contained one million coding-agent trajectories. Most alerts followed a misunderstood task or an overly persistent pursuit of the goal.

The ToolPrivBench researchers grouped 3,600 tools by privilege and tested eleven models. Six crossed a 30% rate of unnecessary high-privilege choices. A temporary server error often pushed an agent toward an administrative route even though a narrower tool could still finish the job.

After repeated failures, a prompt to choose the narrow tool lost force. The researchers added temporary-error trajectories to training and rewarded work inside the safer tool set. After training, unnecessary privilege use measured 39.7%, 27.0%, and 18.9% across three Qwen variants. General-task performance remained within 95.8% to 100.2% of the starting level.

PlanBench-XL created another kind of break. The required tool disappeared, returned plausible nonsense, or was replaced by a similar function. Across 327 tasks, an agent searched among 1,665 tools. Every task required at least five calls, and a working alternative always existed.

GPT-5.4 fell from 51.9% accuracy without disruption to 11.36% under the hardest blocking condition. Plausible wrong answers caused the most damage. Extra attempts gave most models less than five percentage points. After an action, a financial agent checks the state: the entry exists in the right ledger, the amount reconciles, the version remains current, and the permission stays within its boundary. The next step opens after this check.

An accounting agent receives four permissions. The first opens invoice reading. The others allow preparation of an entry, saving a draft, and posting. Each is limited to one client, a volume, and a short lifetime. A dangerous tool opens at the approved escalation point. The alert shows a person the agent's path and the possible damage.

How the economics change

Story 03

In Claude Code, 15% of novices and 28–33% of experts reached a fully verified result

Anthropic studied roughly 400,000 Claude Code sessions. People made about 70% of the decisions about goals and plans. Claude made about 80% of the execution decisions.

One novice instruction triggered about five agent actions and 600 words of output. For an expert, the averages reached twelve actions and 3,200 words. Fully verified success rose from 15% for novices to 28% to 33% for intermediate and expert users. After serious trouble, session success climbed from 4% to 15% as the user's domain expertise increased.

The largest difference appeared between a novice and a competent practitioner. Coding background mattered less. Anthropic describes an accountant opening Python for the first time, specifying the reconciliation rules, catching a missed edge case, and steering the work to completion.

Dwarkesh Patel connects the persistence of this effect to training data. A new model skill requires vast numbers of domain examples. Quality criteria and correct solution paths must accompany them. Common work can supply this material in advance. A rare exception brings fresh human analysis.

On June 17, Moody's published its first finance skills in the open SKILL.md format. Each skill is a file that tells the agent how to run an analysis and recognize a good result. An MCP server feeds it ratings, research, and market data. The same file works in different compatible AI platforms.

A firm can keep this file separately from the model and add verified cases as they appear. It records how the firm reads revenue and performs reconciliations, including the way it handles exceptions. The working version of the skill survives a model change. Verified examples stay with the skill, along with permission to read the required data.

An expert correction is stored with the source data and selected rule. The record keeps the decision rationale, expected state, and verification result. The next similar case enters the evaluation set. A confirmed rule enters the firm's policy.

A senior specialist sets the plan and completion criterion. The agent works through the chain, and mid-level staff carry more clients. A partner's rare case travels across the group through verified examples.

Story 04

Pilot launched Meridian after 187,000 month-end closes

Pilot's Meridian runs the month-end close and returns finished books for an accountant's approval. The product writes results into the ledger, records each decision, and brings forward the calls that need judgment.

Pilot built Meridian inside its own accounting practice and used it from 2017. The external product appeared after more than 187,000 monthly closes across 8,000 businesses.

Over that period, the system collected firm policies and client processes. Its history retained edge cases, checks, and accepted decisions. Meridian can close historical periods and writes completed output directly into the general ledger. QuickBooks Online has the deepest support; Xero and NetSuite are also connected.

In a group of accounting firms, an acquired practice becomes a production lab. A new case enters the shared format with its decision rationale. Each firm's rules keep their own version, so separate client policies remain distinct.

Meridian presents a $1 million book of business per accountant as the target. A test across a few clients quickly reveals real capacity. The team measures review minutes and material corrections after approval. Reopened periods, active clients per accountant, and gross margin receive their own lines.

BCG reports finance teams that automated 90% of reporting and reached 80% touchless invoice processing. More than 30% of team capacity moved into advisory work. In a group of accounting firms, capacity is first tested inside the close, with exception rules kept by each practice.

Gusto divided firm growth across six agents. The first reviews the website and finds prospects before a message is drafted. Another reads the firm's market position. The remaining agents build an expansion offer and calculate its price.

Patrice Malloy spent about an hour researching one prospect. Her agent now finds the company and contact, pulls recent news, and drafts a message in minutes. The accountant decides whether to send it. Another agent calculates expected margin before an expansion offer reaches an existing client.

Story 05

In AA-Briefcase, average task cost varied by more than 800×

AA-Briefcase sent models through 91 tasks inside messy, multi-week business projects. The average task cost ranged from about $0.04 for DeepSeek V4 Flash to more than $31 for Claude Fable 5. The spread exceeded 800 times.

The leading model satisfied every requirement in 3% of tasks. On 31 tasks, every tested model scored below 50% of the rubric. GLM-5.2 landed about 90 Elo behind Claude Opus 4.8 at less than one quarter of the cost. A model-selection table is built from evidence on the job class it will handle.

For vendor invoices, such a table records the price of an accepted result together with rework. An exception brings in a stronger model, and the delay enters the cost. Ordinary code checks an irreversible entry before the accountant approves it.

Context carries its own bill. Deloitte reports that enterprise software vendors are introducing fees and restrictions around access to customer-created data. Repeated reads from an accounting or sales system can turn a familiar subscription into a nonlinear cost. Before launch, the company checks existing contracts for programmatic access rights.

At LangChain, one heavy user could spend several thousand dollars on coding agents in a week. The company routed Claude Code, Codex, and its own agents through one gateway so the cost became visible before the bill arrived.

A budget can belong to the company, workspace, person, or key. Its window may cover an hour, day, week, or month. Each charge connects to an agent, its action history, and a failure. Warnings arrive before a hard limit. Budget increases remain in the log, and a separate report finds applications that bypassed the gateway.

In the first AI-accounting pilot, total agent cost enters gross margin per client. One representative month travels through the same path the agent will later use. The financial model counts context calls and retries inside the completed process. The contract specifies bulk export, access to updates, rate limits, history, and a working exit path.
