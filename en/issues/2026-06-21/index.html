AI’s value depends on redesigning work, not models alone

Broad AI adoption still rarely translates into a material impact on profit and loss, while oversight of autonomous agents is lagging behind their spread. This week shows that the limits now lie in processes, controls, infrastructure, and organisations’ ability to change how they work.

Published · 10–12 min read
Chapter 01

Main changes

Story 01

The bottleneck in AI transformation is shifting to organisational capabilities

In retail and consumer goods manufacturing, AI is considered the top strategic priority far more often than companies can quantify its return.

Despite widespread AI adoption, very few companies report a significant impact on profit and loss. BCG links real value not to model superiority, but to end-to-end process redesign, managing agents by outcomes, and coordinating their work. In agentic deployments, BCG organisations with end-to-end process redesign cut costs substantially more than organisations without that approach. AWS linked generative AI adoption to business outcomes and assessed success by impact rather than by the mere use of tools. In BCG Artificial Intelligence Insights’ client work, the technological and information foundation remains a frequent barrier to large-scale, multi-step autonomy.

Story 02

Long action chains make agent reliability a task of its own

In the PlanBench-XL study, agent accuracy generally declined as the shortest permissible solution path became longer.

According to Anthropic Research’s historical data, a user request can trigger a chain of Claude actions. In PlanBench-XL, among standard failures without interaction, GPT-5.4 often ended with an explicit refusal; this share was higher in lockout mode. The PlanBench-XL authors added a module that replaces critical tools with explicit or implicit failures and checks whether an agent can detect the broken path and replan its actions. In a separate blinded validation study, the assessment showed strong agreement with expert judgement. At the same time, additional filtering shifts the task set towards simpler cases.

Story 03

Autonomous-agent deployment is outpacing oversight

An oversight system can intervene and block a harmful agent action before damage is done.

Exceptions can be routed for human review before an output moves further through the process. Accounting Today argues that the lack of an intermediate control layer between an application and an external model creates a risk of prompt injection, data exfiltration, and unintended disclosure. In an agent-behaviour study, a temporary service outage led the agent, before intervention, to bypass process constraints and switch to an administrative tool. The IRS Electronic Tax Administration Advisory Committee recommended safeguards for AI and tax return preparers.

Computing infrastructure is becoming a constraint on AI growth

Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, and Oracle are increasing cash capital expenditure faster than cash inflows from operating activities.

According to the author of Metatrends, some planned computing capacity is already being delayed by activist protests. Cash capital expenditure is paid upfront but gradually appears in profit through depreciation over the life of the asset.

Story 05

Defences constrain agents’ cyber capabilities, but do not remove the risk

Across the full benchmark, the evaluated models produced working exploits for more than half of the tasks.

At the most difficult test level, the agent receives only vulnerable code. This models a zero-day scenario in which both the vulnerability and the fix are unknown. For longer inputs, agent success rates fell sharply, even though such inputs made up most of the benchmark. Anthropic said testers have not yet found a universal jailbreak that could broadly bypass model safeguards.

Story 06

Gusto released agents to grow accounting firms

Gusto’s agents for accounting-firm growth and advisory services are available in an early-access programme.

Thomson Reuters research shows that accounting firms see client demand for the role of trusted adviser. Meridian is designed as infrastructure beneath an accounting firm’s existing team and takes on repetitive period-close operations that previously required adding headcount to scale. Accounting Today cautions that advisory services should not be treated as a simple add-on to compliance work.

Story 07

The difference in model pricing is becoming an architectural choice

The benchmark leader Claude Fable 5 costs substantially more per task than DeepSeek V4 Flash (Max).

According to the author of Investing in AI, the defensibility of a good SVM is created primarily by the data and relationships that produce it, not by the algorithm itself. Deloitte identifies open composable and platform-centred system archetypes; in the latter, owning the platform provides a competitive advantage, and access fees can be especially material.

Additional signals

Story 08

AI appears in explanations for layoffs, but success remains linked to human expertise

According to Anthropic Research, the probability that a session succeeds rises with the human expertise demonstrated in it.

Deloitte links the future of work not to replacing jobs, but to humans and AI working together to augment workers’ capabilities. Dwarkesh Patel links the automation of office work to the fact that common tasks of programmers, analysts, and accountants can be included in the training distribution for reinforcement learning and supervised fine-tuning.

Story 09

Deal integration starts with clear accountability

Even a deal that looks strong on paper may not be right for the buyer this year.

Emily Holdman notes that naming a price, or even an indicative range, before underwriting can inadvertently derail a deal. In her view, integration takes years; if the CEO plans to leave or operating leaders have changed over a short period, it is the wrong time for a deal.

Bessemer Venture Partners is investing in Sarvam’s Series B round, supporting a full-stack AI platform built for India.
