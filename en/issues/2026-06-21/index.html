Chapter 01

Rebuilding the work

Story 01

A team needs free capacity to rebuild a process

BCG traces the gap between 70% adoption and 16% significant value to the design of daily work.

When an agent enters an old process, it speeds up one section. The queue then hits the same exception route, leaving total productivity gains at 10% to 20%. BCG's first end-to-end programs delivered three times the productivity. Cycle time fell by as much as 80%. Long-term cost fell by at least 60%.

A European bank rebuilt loan processing around the completed decision. Its agent recognizes documents, synchronizes data, checks for fraud, and validates signatures. Automation covered more than 90% of consumer loans and over 70% of mortgages. Process productivity rose by more than 50%.

AWS tied AI work to features that reached customers. Experienced peer engineers joined teams and changed everyday habits alongside them. The redesign then moved into planning, maintenance, and operations, since coding accounts for about one third of the work. BCG reports 27% more features shipped.

BCG recommends focusing the redesign on two or three domains that move a business metric. For an accounting firm, that unit is one closed month for one client. A named owner carries the work from document collection through approval, and the team gets time to rebuild the exception route. Each week it measures elapsed close time, review minutes, reopened work, and margin per client.

Story 02

Every agent action gets its own permission

A separate control layer gives an agent permission for one action and sets an expiry. Access disappears when the work is done.

Deloitte embedded this layer into a lending workflow. Supervising agents watch the trajectory, inspect the result, and stop a risky action during execution. Each check has a defined target, an evaluation rule, and an escalation route. Ordinary code handles stable rules. A model reads situations whose meaning depends on context.

Google DeepMind builds access as a trust ladder. Verified behavior unlocks a wider scope. Reading and other reversible actions can enter an asynchronous review queue. Data deletion and infrastructure changes wait for a check before execution. The monitor is measured by traffic coverage, caught behavior, and response time. Its first training set contained one million coding-agent trajectories. Most alerts appeared when an agent misread the task or chased the goal too eagerly.

The ToolPrivBench researchers grouped 3,600 tools by privilege and tested eleven models. Six crossed a 30% rate of unnecessary high-privilege choices. A temporary server error often pushed an agent toward an administrative route even though a narrower tool could still finish the job.

After repeated failures, a prompt to choose the narrow tool lost force. The researchers added temporary-error trajectories to training and rewarded attempts to stay inside the safer tool set. After training, unnecessary privilege use measured 39.7%, 27.0%, and 18.9% across three Qwen variants. General-task performance remained within 95.8% to 100.2% of the starting level.

An accounting agent receives four separate grants to read an invoice, prepare an entry, save a draft, and post it. Each grant carries a client boundary, a volume limit, and a short lifetime. The dangerous tool remains unavailable until the approved path reaches its escalation point. A human receives the trajectory and the expected damage radius with the alert.

Chapter 02

The new economics

Story 03

Meridian turns month-end close into review-ready work

Pilot's Meridian runs the month-end close and returns finished books for an accountant's approval. The product writes results into the ledger, records each decision, and brings forward the calls that need judgment.

Pilot says Meridian was refined across more than 187,000 monthly closes for 8,000 businesses. It follows the firm's accounting policies and each client's process. QuickBooks Online receives the deepest support today, with Xero and NetSuite also connected. The firm keeps its ledger and avoids a data migration.

Meridian presents a $1 million book of business per accountant as the target shape. A pilot can test that claim quickly. Measure review minutes per client, material corrections after approval, reopened closes, active clients per accountant, and gross margin. A review process that repeats the full close will show up in the first two measures.

Gusto divided firm growth across six agents. The chain starts with a website review and prospect search, then drafts the message. It moves on to the firm's market position, builds an expansion offer, and calculates the price.

Patrice Malloy says research for one prospect took about an hour. Her agent now finds the company and contact, pulls recent news, and drafts the message in minutes. Sending waits for her approval. Another agent calculates expected margin before an expansion offer reaches an existing client.

BCG reports finance teams that automated 90% of reporting and reached 80% touchless invoice processing. More than 30% of team capacity moved into advisory work. In an accounting roll-up, the sequence starts by proving capacity inside the close. Exception policy stays attached to each firm, and growth agents receive the newly available hours.

Story 04

The same agent task can cost 800 times as much

AA-Briefcase sent models through 91 tasks inside messy, multi-week business projects. The average task cost ranged from about $0.04 for DeepSeek V4 Flash to more than $31 for Claude Fable 5. The spread exceeded 800 times.

The leading model satisfied every requirement in 3% of tasks. On 31 tasks, every tested model scored below 50% of the rubric. GLM-5.2 landed about 90 Elo behind Claude Opus 4.8 at less than one quarter of the cost. A useful routing table needs evidence from the actual job class.

The table for vendor invoices records the price of an accepted result together with rework. When the close hits an exception, the route brings in a stronger model and counts the delay. Ordinary code checks an irreversible entry before the accountant approves it. The economics cover the entire accepted task.

Context carries its own bill. Deloitte reports that software vendors are putting fees and new restrictions around access to data their customers created. Repeated reads from an accounting or sales system can turn a familiar subscription into a nonlinear cost. Existing vendor contracts need a review of programmatic access rights before the agent reaches production.

At LangChain, one heavy user could spend several thousand dollars on coding agents in a week. The company routed Claude Code, Codex, and its own agents through one gateway so the cost became visible before the bill arrived.

A budget can belong to the company, workspace, person, or key. Its window can cover an hour, day, week, or month. Each charge connects to an agent, trace, and failure. Warnings arrive before a hard limit, and budget increases stay in the log. A separate report finds applications that passed around the gateway.

In AI accounting, total agent cost enters gross margin per client in the first pilot. One representative month should travel through the same pipe the agent will use. The commercial model counts context calls and retries inside the completed workflow. The contract spells out bulk export, incremental access, rate limits, history, and a usable exit path.
