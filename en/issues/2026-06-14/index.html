AI speeds up execution, but does not remove the need for verification, accountability, and work design

According to Bessemer research, 90% of technology and engineering teams are adopting AI or making it central to their operations. Yet fewer than 10% of surveyed organizations have achieved meaningful financial gains from generative AI, and as models become more capable, an error increasingly looks like work that was completed but completed incorrectly.

Published · 10–12 min read
Chapter 01

Main changes

Story 01

Stronger agents complete work more often, but may complete it incorrectly

In the Workflow-GYM study, even the strongest models succeed on only a little over 30% of tasks.

The Workflow-GYM authors note that as a process grows longer, it becomes substantially harder to maintain coherent planning and reasoning throughout the trajectory. If execution deviates from its intended trajectory, later actions can appear locally plausible while relying on false assumptions; without recovery, that deviation spreads further and leads to task failure. In CODA-BENCH, all models perform substantially worse on the CODA-HARD subset than on the full benchmark.

Even the best evaluated agents fail to find the right data in nearly 20% of tasks. Kimi-K2.6 uses 39.4 rounds yet reaches only 43.8% EA: more interactions alone do not remove the model's fundamental limitations. In Rob May's view, Anthropic and other companies are separating the executing agent from an independent evaluating agent; it is easier to tune a separate skeptical evaluator than to make a generator reliably assess its own output.

Story 02

AI adoption does not create an advantage without redesigning work

Fewer than 10% of surveyed organizations have achieved meaningful financial gains from generative AI.

According to Bessemer research, 90% of technology and engineering teams are adopting AI or making it central to their operations. In Deloitte's survey, a substantial share of respondents expected efficiency gains of 20–60%, but a substantial share reported gains below 10%. Fewer than 30% of responding organizations are actively redesigning work, roles, and career paths around AI. About 59% of organizations cite data quality and governance as key barriers to generative AI adoption.

64% of surveyed workers report lower trust in agentic AI. Farhan Thawar, Shopify's vice president and head of engineering, standardized a shared infrastructure layer for AI tools rather than selecting one tool. In Rob May's assessment, AI-generated tests for checking functional behavior are not yet reliable enough to eliminate human review.

Story 03

AI compresses execution, but does not replace engineering judgment

Demand for junior roles, in Arvind Narayanan's assessment, is weakening sharply.

The SWE-chat study found that only 44% of agent-generated code remains in user commits. As agent-assisted development spreads, engineers are finding that supervising coding agents takes an unexpected amount of time. Among Shopify's senior engineers, a pattern is emerging in which several AI agents work in parallel on different parts of the codebase, while engineers review the results, discard those that do not work, and combine those that do.

The authors of AI as Normal Technology argue that the barrier is not learning syntax, but the skilled judgment required for good decisions while retaining accountability. Naoko Takeda, a data specialist on the Cash App team, wrote that Block “imposed AI on everyone,” yet she observed “very limited productivity gains.” In every case reviewed by the authors of AI as Normal Technology involving software-developer layoffs allegedly caused by AI, they found the same mismatch with the stated explanation.

Story 04

AI for financial reporting needs a clear audit trail

A survey found that 90% of tax professionals use publicly available models such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, or Claude.

In Magnetic's description, results are cross-checked by several models to create a confidence score for each value, available to subsequent agents and human reviewers. During document parsing, the system reads printed fields, handwritten notes, margin annotations, circled and underlined items, and metadata including names, the number of files, and file types. The system extracts a figure from a K-1 form, links it to the prior year's database table, applies the relevant Tax Code section, and records the result in the firm's software.

When complying with SOX, internal-audit policies, or external-reporting requirements, organizations must be able to show how numbers were produced and demonstrate compliance with approved procedures. The author of a16z News predicts a new kind of accounting system built on a company's living context.

Story 05

Faster exploit creation narrows the former window for fixes

Microsoft placed 14 of 21 assessed vulnerabilities in the “Exploitation Less Likely” or “Exploitation Unlikely” categories; Mythos Preview created PoCs for 13 of the 14.

According to Anthropic's analysis, Mythos Preview substantially outperformed earlier models at creating exploits for arbitrary code execution and often did so with minimal information about vulnerabilities. Palo Alto Networks states that frontier models such as Mythos Preview and OpenAI models completed the equivalent of a year's penetration-testing work in under three weeks. After an update is published, attackers can compare the pre-patch source code or binary with the new version, identify the changes, and reconstruct the vulnerability; a working exploit is therefore often only a matter of time.

In several trials, Opus 4.8 came close to creating an exploit, producing arbitrary read and write primitives and finding a KASLR leak, but in this test environment it could not link them into a lowpriv-to-SYSTEM escalation chain. Anthropic estimates that as the cost of turning a fix into an attack tool approaches zero, industrial control systems, medical devices, and Internet of Things devices will become even more vulnerable.

Additional signals

Story 06

Agentic commerce moves product choice ahead of the interface

Decisions are increasingly made before a consumer reaches search results, a marketplace, or an app.

Deloitte links this shift to AI agents increasingly making purchase decisions: traditional marketing and brand recognition are no longer enough. If an AI agent cannot reliably interpret, compare, or trust product data, a product can quietly disappear from recommendations without a human rejection and without warning signals in existing KPIs. If a brand does not show a clear advantage on attributes that matter to agents, it will be treated as interchangeable.

Accenture points to a risk of losing differentiation: attention and scale may matter less than reliable machine-readable signals. In Accenture's assessment, platforms built to capture attention must now compete for influence over decisions made earlier in AI-mediated environments.

Story 07

In deals, a checklist does not replace integration experience

M&A Science links the outcome of complex deals to the experience of people who have already encountered similar breakdowns; one example is Vensure Employer Solutions, which has completed 105 HR acquisitions since 2004.

In an M&A Science episode, participants discuss situations where formal diligence has already ended but the deal is still threatened by seller conflicts, cross-border constraints, or post-close surprises. The episode page describes Vensure Employer Solutions as an HR company operating in more than 160 countries and having completed 105 HR acquisitions since 2004. That figure shows the scale of one operator's experience, but does not prove that its approach to integration suits every company.

Story 08

No equivalent of a network effect has been shown for LLMs

In the No Priors episode with Mark Zuckerberg, the view was expressed that the industry remains on its trajectory; that view supports the rationale for major investment if the trajectory holds.

In the a16z interview with Benedict Evans, no equivalent of a network effect comparable to Instagram, YouTube, or Google Search is shown for LLMs. In the No Priors episode with Mark Zuckerberg, confidence in major investment is tied to the view that the industry remains on its trajectory.
